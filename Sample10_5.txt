The evolution of originality
Defining Human creativity in the
age of Generative AI
In 2026, Generative AI has become a corner-
-stone of technological advancement in human
history with the wide spread use of Gener-
-ative AI, what used to be a time-taking task
is now done with a prompt and a few clicks
of a button. Everything from image creation,
video generation, and fetching information to
making creative new ideas have become
something anyone can do. The tremedous
growth in the capabilities of Generative AI has
led to some arguing that its convenience
has led to dulling of human creativity.
However, unlike AI, human creativity isn't just
weights and biases in a neural network AI
can only build upon existing data to predict
suitable output, but the ability to imagine
beyond what already exists is unique to
humans, Human creativity stems from
experience, emotions and imagination without
a data set to train from Generative AI systems
that only train on a human produced dataset,
can't invent something that doesn't follow
the strict pattern that the data set trained it to
to recognise, these systems can only recombine
what it has learnt to provide a merely "satis-
-fying" output when an AI generates a poem, it
is only a string of words that it "predicts" will
go together well and if such a poem can move
a person, then the question is "does originality lie
in the process, or the product?"
Rather than viewing AI as the bane of all human
originality, we view it as a catalyst to redefine
originality. Originality is not about just producing
something, it is about the transformation of experi-
-ance into meaning. Human originality lies in pur-
-pose belief and identity. It is about the emotions,
pain, the struggle that a human experiances and
conveys it as a poem, much of what human value in
great pieces of art is the emotion behind it, the hard-
-work and effort of the artist and the meaning it conveys;
these details are what make art difficult to produce.
It has meaning since it cost the creator something.
